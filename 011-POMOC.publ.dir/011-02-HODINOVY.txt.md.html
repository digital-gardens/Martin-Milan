Kaarel Kaljurand’s 2007 doctoral dissertation explores Attempto Controlled English (ACE) as a user-friendly bridge for the Semantic Web. While formal languages like OWL and SWRL are mathematically precise, their steep learning curve and complex syntax often alienate domain experts who lack training in logic. This research demonstrates that ACE can serve as a bidirectional interface, allowing users to write and understand ontologies through a restricted subset of natural English. By mapping ACE to Discourse Representation Structures and first-order logic, the author ensures that these texts remain computationally decidable and compatible with existing reasoners. The thesis ultimately argues that using controlled natural languages improves the learnability and accessibility of web-based knowledge representation. This approach empowers users to "think aloud" in plain text while maintaining the rigorous standards required for automated machine processing.
