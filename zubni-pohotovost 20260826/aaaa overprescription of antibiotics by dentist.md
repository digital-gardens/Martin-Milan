Overprescription of antibiotics by dentists is a major public health concern, with studies showing that up to 80% of antibiotic prescriptions written in dental settings may be unnecessary or inappropriate.

 +1
Why Overprescription Happens
Treating Pain Instead of Infection: Antibiotics do not cure toothaches or pulpitis (inflammation of the tooth pulp) unless a spreading systemic infection is present. However, they are frequently prescribed just in case or for immediate pain relief. 

National Institutes of Health (NIH) | (.gov)
 +3
Skipping Local Treatment: Conditions like periapical abscesses require local mechanical interventions—such as root canals, tooth extractions, or surgical drainage—rather than systemic drugs. 

MDPI
Patient Pressure and Habits: Dentists sometimes prescribe antibiotics because patients demand them, or out of defensive, routine habit from older training guidelines.

Risks and Dangers
Antibiotic Resistance: Overuse accelerates the growth of drug-resistant superbugs, making future serious infections harder to treat.

 +1
Side Effects: Unneeded medications expose patients to severe allergic reactions, nausea, and Clostridioides difficile (C. diff) intestinal infections. 

CIDRAP
Wrong Drugs: High-risk drugs like clindamycin are still overprescribed in dentistry despite guidelines advising safer alternatives due to severe side-effect risks.

When Antibiotics Are Actually Needed
Systemic Signs: Dentists should only prescribe antibiotics if there is fever, swollen lymph nodes, facial swelling, malaise, or rapid spreading of the infection. 

MDPI
Vulnerable Patients: Prophylaxis is strictly meant for high-risk patients (such as those with specific heart conditions or severe immunocompromise) undergoing invasive procedures.
