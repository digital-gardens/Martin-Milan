A **battery** converts [chemical energy](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_energy "Chemical energy") into [electrical energy](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_energy "Electrical energy") by a [chemical reaction](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_reaction "Chemical reaction"). Usually the chemicals are kept inside the battery. It is used in a [circuit](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit "Circuit") to power other [components](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Component "Component"). A battery produces [direct current](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_current "Direct current") (DC) electricity (electricity that flows in one direction, and does not switch back and forth).

Using the [electricity](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity "Electricity") from an outlet in a building is cheaper and [more efficient](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_efficiency "Energy efficiency"), but a battery can provide electricity in areas that do not have [electric power distribution](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_power_distribution "Electric power distribution"). It is also useful for things that move, such as [electric vehicles](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_vehicle "Electric vehicle") and [mobile phones](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone "Mobile phone").

Batteries may be [primary](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_cell "Primary cell") or [secondary](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_cell "Secondary cell"). The primary is thrown away when it can no longer provide electricity. The secondary can be recharged and reused.

## The chemistry inside a battery

A Battery can be one *cell* or many *cells*. Each cell has an [anode](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anode "Anode"), [cathode](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathode "Cathode") and [electrolyte](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrolyte "Electrolyte"). The electrolyte is the main material inside the battery. It is often a type of acid, and can be dangerous to touch. The anode reacts with the electrolyte to produce [electrons](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrons "Electrons") (this is the *negative* or *\-* end). The cathode reacts with the electrolyte and takes electrons (this is the *positive* or *+* end).[^1] An [electric current](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_current "Electric current") happens when a wire connects the anode to the cathode, and the electrons move from one end to the other. (But a battery can be damaged by just a wire connecting the two ends, so a *load* is also needed between the two ends. The load is something that slows the electrons down, and usually does something useful, like a [lightbulb](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightbulb "Lightbulb") in a [flashlight](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flashlight "Flashlight"), or the [electronics](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics "Electronics") in a [calculator](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calculator "Calculator")).[^2]

![](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Generadores_en_paralelo--batteries_parallel.png)

Batteries connected in parallel - shown in a schematic and a drawing

The electrolyte can be [liquid](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid "Liquid") or [solid](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid "Solid"). A battery is called a [wet cell](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_cell "Wet cell") or [dry cell](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_cell "Dry cell") battery, depending on the type of electrolyte.

The [chemical reactions](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_reaction "Chemical reaction") that occur in a battery are [exothermic](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exothermic "Exothermic") reactions. This type of reaction makes heat. For example, if you leave your laptop on for a long time, and then touch the battery, it will be warm or hot.

A [rechargeable battery](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rechargeable_battery "Rechargeable battery") is recharged by reversing the chemical reaction that occurs within the battery. But a rechargeable battery can only be recharged a given number of times (recharge life). Even built-in batteries, cannot be recharged forever. Moreover, each time a battery is recharged, its ability to hold a charge goes down a bit. Non-rechargeable batteries should not be charged as various harmful substances can leak out, such as [potassium hydroxide](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium_hydroxide "Potassium hydroxide").

The cells can be connected to make a bigger battery. Connecting the positive of one cell to the negative of the next cell is called connecting them *in series*. The voltage of each battery are added together. Two six volt batteries connected in series will make 12 volts.[^3]

Connecting the positive of one cell to the positive of the other, and the negative to the negative is called connecting them *in parallel*. The voltage stays the same, but the current is added together. Voltage is the pressure pushing the electrons through the wires, it is measured in [volts](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volt "Volt"). Current is how many electrons can go at once, it is measured in [amps](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampere "Ampere"). The combination of current and voltage is the power (watts = volts x amps) of the battery.

## Battery sizes

Batteries come in many different shapes, sizes and [voltages](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltage "Voltage").

AA, AAA, C, and D cells, including [alkaline batteries](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkaline_battery "Alkaline battery"), are of standard sizes and shapes, and have about 1.5 volts. The voltage of a cell depends on the chemicals used. The [electric charge](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_charge "Electric charge") it can supply depends on how large the cell is, as well as what chemicals. The charge a battery delivers is usually measured in [ampere-hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampere_hour "en:Ampere hour"). Since the voltage stays the same, more charge means a bigger cell can supply more amps, or run for a longer time.

## History

The first battery was invented in [1800](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/1800 "1800") by [Alessandro Volta](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Volta "Alessandro Volta"). Nowadays, his battery is called the [voltaic pile](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltaic_pile "Voltaic pile"). [^4]

In small, modern batteries, the fluid is immobilized in a kind of paste and everything is put in a sealed case. Because of this case, nothing can spill out of the battery. Larger batteries, such as car batteries, still have liquid inside and are not sealed. A kind of battery that uses molten salts as the electrolyte was invented during World War II.

## Alternatives to batteries

[Fuel cells](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_cell "Fuel cell") and [solar cells](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_cell "Solar cell") are not batteries because they do not store the energy inside them.

A [capacitor](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacitor "Capacitor") is not a battery because it does not store the energy in a chemical reaction. A capacitor can store electricity and create electricity much faster than a battery, but it usually costs too much to make it as big as a battery can be. Scientists and [chemical engineers](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering "Chemical engineering") are working to make better capacitors and batteries for [electric cars](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_vehicle "Electric vehicle").

Small [electrical generator](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_generator "Electrical generator") s operated by hand and foot can supply power in small electrical devices. [Clockwork radios](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clockwork_radio "Clockwork radio"), [clockwork torches](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clockwork_torch "Clockwork torch") and similar devices also have a wind-up spring to store [mechanical energy](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_energy "Mechanical energy").
