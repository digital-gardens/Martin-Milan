Pure mathematics is an informal term to describe the study of the properties and structure of abstract objects, 1 such as the Mandelbrot set. This may be done without focusing on concrete applications of concepts in the physical world.

In the context of the [philosophy of mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophy_of_mathematics "Philosophy of mathematics"), **pure mathematics** is an informal term to describe the study of mathematical concepts independently of any application outside [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics"). These concepts may originate in real-world concerns, and the results obtained may later turn out to be useful for practical applications, but [research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_research "Mathematical research") is not primarily motivated by such applications. Instead, the appeal is attributed to the intellectual challenge and [aesthetic beauty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_beauty "Mathematical beauty") of defining new mathematical objects or working out the mathematical consequences of basic principles.

While the distinction between pure and [applied mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematics "Applied mathematics") has existed since at least [ancient Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece "Ancient Greece"), the concept was elaborated upon around the year 1900,[^2] after the introduction of theories with counter-intuitive properties (such as [non-Euclidean geometries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Euclidean_geometries "Non-Euclidean geometries") and [Cantor's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Cantor "Georg Cantor") theory of infinite sets), and the discovery of apparent paradoxes (such as [continuous functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_function "Continuous function") that are nowhere [differentiable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Differentiable_function "Differentiable function"), and [Russell's paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell's_paradox "Russell's paradox")). This introduced the need to renew the concept of [mathematical rigor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_rigor "Mathematical rigor") and rewrite all mathematics accordingly, with a systematic use of [axiomatic methods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_method "Axiomatic method").

Nevertheless, almost all mathematical theories remained motivated by problems coming from the real world or from less abstract mathematical theories. Also, many mathematical theories, which had seemed to be totally pure mathematics, were eventually used in applied areas, mainly [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") and [computer science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_science "Computer science"). A famous early example is [Isaac Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaac_Newton "Isaac Newton") 's demonstration that his [law of universal gravitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_universal_gravitation "Law of universal gravitation") implied that [planets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet "Planet") move in orbits that are [conic sections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conic_section "Conic section"), geometrical curves that had been studied in antiquity by [Apollonius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollonius_of_Perga "Apollonius of Perga"). Another example is the problem of [factoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factorization "Factorization") large [integers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integer "Integer"), which is the basis of the [RSA cryptosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_cryptosystem "RSA cryptosystem"), widely used to secure [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet "Internet") communications.[^3]

It follows that, currently, the distinction between pure and [applied mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematics "Applied mathematics") is more a philosophical point of view or preference rather than a rigid subdivision of mathematics.[^4]

## History

### Ancient Greece

#### Mathematical Platonism

Ancient Greek mathematicians were amongst the earliest to make a distinction between pure and applied mathematics. [Plato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plato "Plato") helped to create the gap between "arithmetic", now called [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory"), and "logistic", now called [arithmetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arithmetic "Arithmetic"). Plato regarded logistic (arithmetic) as appropriate for businessmen and men of war who "must learn the art of numbers or \[they\] will not know how to array \[their\] troops" and arithmetic (number theory) as appropriate for philosophers "because \[they have\] to arise out of the sea of change and lay hold of true being." [^5] In this wise [Euclid of Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid_of_Alexandria "Euclid of Alexandria"), when asked by one of his students of what use was the study of geometry, asked his slave to give the student threepence, "since he must make gain of what he learns." [^6] The Greek mathematician [Apollonius of Perga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollonius_of_Perga "Apollonius of Perga"), asked about the usefulness of some of his theorems in Book IV of *Conics*, asserted that [^7]

> They are worthy of acceptance for the sake of the demonstrations themselves, in the same way as we accept many other things in mathematics for this and for no other reason.

And since many of his results were not applicable to the science or engineering of his day, Apollonius further argued in the preface of the fifth book of *Conics* that the subject is one of those that "...seem worthy of study for their own sake." [^7]

#### Aristotle

It is worth noting that in ancient Greek philosophy, an experimental approach of mathematics and no real distinction between [mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics "Mathematics") and [physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") was already present in authors such as [Thales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales "Thales") and [Archimedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimedes "Archimedes"), and for example in Aristotle and his school.[^8] This duality of [platonism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plato "Plato") vs [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle "Aristotle") is still present in modern mathematics.[^9]

Different historical records of the school of [Pythagoras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagoras "Pythagoras") show this contradiction too from elements of [mysticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysticism "Mysticism") similar to [Plato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plato "Plato") to the proof [irrationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrational_numbers "Irrational numbers") which leads to the [paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradox "Paradox") that an infinite non repetitive set of digits cannot belong to the "finite" and "well defined" world of [Euclidean geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_geometry "Euclidean geometry"). Last but not least [Zeno paradox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeno_paradox "Zeno paradox") shows again this duality between the pure logical reasoning (splitting distance by two is always possible) and the applied realm (the fact that the traveler never arrives).

### 19th century

#### Pure mathematics invented

The term "pure mathematics" itself is enshrined in the full title of the [Sadleirian Chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadleirian_Professor_of_Pure_Mathematics "Sadleirian Professor of Pure Mathematics"), "Sadleirian Professor of Pure Mathematics", founded (as a professorship) in the mid-nineteenth century. The idea of a separate discipline of *pure* mathematics may have emerged at that time. The generation of [Carl Friedrich Gauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Friedrich_Gauss "Carl Friedrich Gauss") (1777 to 1855) made no sweeping distinction of the kind between *pure* and *applied*. In the following years, specialisation and professionalisation (particularly in the [Weierstrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass "Weierstrass") approach to [mathematical analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_analysis "Mathematical analysis")) started to make a rift more apparent.

#### The problem of infinities

After [Weierstrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass "Weierstrass"), by the end of 19th century, an important discussion about the role of infinities came from authors like [Gregor Cantor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Cantor "Gregor Cantor") and early examples of [fractals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor_Set "Cantor Set") and [chaos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weierstrass_function "Weierstrass function"). [Ludwig Wittgenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Wittgenstein "Ludwig Wittgenstein") considered Cantor's approach with [uncountable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncountable "Uncountable") sets the cancer of mathematics.[^10] [Henri Poincare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Poincare "Henri Poincare") seems to have compared set theory to a temporary disease [^11] Infinities in general are difficult to treat axiomatically, and therefore have been always considered on the fringe of pure mathematics, and this complexity became evident in the works of [Bertrand Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand_Russell "Bertrand Russell") and [Gödel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B6del "Gödel") on [paradoxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradoxes "Paradoxes") in the early 20th century.

### 20th century

At the start of the twentieth century, mathematicians took up the [axiomatic method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiomatic_method "Axiomatic method"), strongly influenced by [David Hilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hilbert "David Hilbert") 's example. The logical formulation of pure mathematics suggested by [Bertrand Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand_Russell "Bertrand Russell") in terms of a [quantifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantifier_\(logic\) "Quantifier (logic)") structure of [propositions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposition_\(mathematics\) "Proposition (mathematics)") seemed more and more plausible, as large parts of mathematics became axiomatised and thus subject to the simple criteria of *[rigorous proof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigorous_proof "Rigorous proof")*.

Pure mathematics, according to a view that can be ascribed to the [Bourbaki group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbaki_group "Bourbaki group"), is what is proved. "Pure mathematician" became a recognized vocation, achievable through training. That said, the case was made pure mathematics is useful in [engineering education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering_education "Engineering education"):[^12]

There is a training in habits of thought, points of view, and intellectual comprehension of ordinary engineering problems, which only the study of higher mathematics can give.

Major advances in the beginning of 20th century was the formalization of [abstract algebra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstract_algebra "Abstract algebra") and [topology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology "Topology"); these two fields were deeply influenced by the pure mathematics philosophy.

#### Finiteness, Number theory and Algebraic geometry

Historically areas often considered attached to pure mathematics are [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory"), where these infinities are typically [countable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countable "Countable") and [algebraic geometry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_geometry "Algebraic geometry") where functions are typically tamed functions [^13] (i.e. [piecewise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piecewise "Piecewise") [polynomial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynomial "Polynomial") or [rational functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_functions "Rational functions")). The success in the proof of [Weil conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weil_conjectures "Weil conjectures") and the unification of these two fields ultimately in the [geometric Langlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_Langlands "Geometric Langlands") program gave momentum to the concept of pure mathematics as a research activity that can self sustain independently. On the other hand, the fields of [functional analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_analysis "Functional analysis"), [partial differential equations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_differential_equations "Partial differential equations"), [statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics "Statistics") and [dynamical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamical_systems "Dynamical systems") were [^14] often considered in the [applied mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_mathematics "Applied mathematics") camp, and this is reflected in the typical organization of mathematics curriculum.[^15]

#### The advance of computers

By the end of the century numerical proofs of the [four color theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_color_theorem "Four color theorem") were primary examples of the advance of computers.[^16] Famous mathematicians such as [Paul Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Cohen "Paul Cohen") already in the 1970s challenged the concept of pure mathematics, stating that there will be a moment in the future that most mathematicians will be replaced by computers.[^17]

#### French vs Russian schools

The French school of mathematics and the western authors in general were deeply influenced by the Bourbaki group and therefore by the philosophical idea to detach mathematics from natural sciences. The Russian school of mathematics instead (e.g. [Kolmogorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolmogorov "Kolmogorov"), [Gelfand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Gelfand "Israel Gelfand") and [Vladimir Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Arnold "Vladimir Arnold")) believed that mathematics is actually fully grounded in experimental sciences such as physics [^18] and biology,[^19] [^20] this was reflected in the separation of the two schools during the [Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_War "Cold War") era due to geopolitical motivations and by the works of authors like [Robert Langlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Langlands "Robert Langlands") which started to piece wise reconnect work from both schools.[^21]

### 21st century

At the beginning of the 21st century, the application of [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") to pure mathematics has attracted the attention of such luminaries as [Ken Ono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Ono "Ken Ono") [^22] and [François Charton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Charton?action=edit&redlink=1 "François Charton (page does not exist)").[^22] [^23] [^24]

Looking at recent research fields such as the Analytic Langlands conjecture [^25] one can see that the distinction between pure and applied blurs again. Another example is the emergence of [chaos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaos_\(mathematics\) "Chaos (mathematics)") in [number theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_theory "Number theory") [^26] [^27] [^28] [^29] [^30] and authors like Robert Langlands advocate for the unification of mathematics with Physics [^31] [^32] through the [Langlands program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langlands_program "Langlands program").

## Pure versus applied mathematics

Mathematicians have always had differing opinions regarding the distinction between pure and applied mathematics. One of the most famous (but perhaps misunderstood) modern examples of this debate can be found in [G.H. Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.H._Hardy "G.H. Hardy") 's 1940 essay *[A Mathematician's Apology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Mathematician's_Apology "A Mathematician's Apology")*.

It is widely believed that Hardy considered applied mathematics to be ugly and dull. Although it is true that Hardy preferred pure mathematics, which he often compared to [painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painting "Painting") and [poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry "Poetry"), Hardy saw the distinction between pure and applied mathematics to be simply that applied mathematics sought to express *physical* truth in a mathematical framework, whereas pure mathematics expressed truths that were independent of the physical world. Hardy made a separate distinction in mathematics between what he called "real" mathematics, "which has permanent aesthetic value", and "the dull and elementary parts of mathematics" that have practical use.[^33]

Hardy considered some physicists, such as [Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein "Albert Einstein") and [Dirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Dirac "Paul Dirac"), to be among the "real" mathematicians, but at the time that he was writing his *Apology*, he considered [general relativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_relativity "General relativity") and [quantum mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantum_mechanics "Quantum mechanics") to be "useless", which allowed him to hold the opinion that only "dull" mathematics was useful. Moreover, Hardy briefly admitted that—just as the application of [matrix theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_\(mathematics\) "Matrix (mathematics)") and [group theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_theory "Group theory") to physics had come about unexpectedly—the time may come where some kinds of beautiful, "real" mathematics may be useful as well.

Another insightful view is offered by American mathematician [Andy Magid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Magid "Andy Magid"):

> I've always thought that a good model here could be drawn from ring theory. In that subject, one has the subareas of [commutative ring theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutative_ring "Commutative ring") and [non-commutative ring theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commutative_ring "Non-commutative ring"). An uninformed observer might think that these represent a dichotomy, but in fact the latter subsumes the former: a non-commutative ring is a not-necessarily-commutative ring. If we use similar conventions, then we could refer to applied mathematics and nonapplied mathematics, where by the latter we *mean not-necessarily-applied mathematics*... \[emphasis added\] [^34]

[Friedrich Engels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Engels "Friedrich Engels") argued in his 1878 book *[Anti-Dühring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-D%C3%BChring "Anti-Dühring")* that "it is not at all true that in pure mathematics the mind deals only with its own creations and imaginations. The concepts of number and figure have not been invented from any source other than the world of reality".[^35]<sup><span title="Page / location: 36">: 36</span> </sup> He further argued that "Before one came upon the idea of deducing the form of a cylinder from the rotation of a rectangle about one of its sides, a number of real rectangles and cylinders, however imperfect in form, must have been examined. Like all other sciences, mathematics arose out of the needs of men...But, as in every department of thought, at a certain stage of development the laws, which were abstracted from the real world, become divorced from the real world, and are set up against it as something independent, as laws coming from outside, to which the world has to conform." [^35]<sup><span title="Page / location: 37">: 37</span></sup>

## In education

After the year 2000,[^36] the case was made by authors like [Cédric Villani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9dric_Villani "Cédric Villani") that pure and abstract mathematics without a [constructivist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constructivism_\(philosophy_of_mathematics\) "Constructivism (philosophy of mathematics)") approach and taking into account other cognitive processes [^37] can become detrimental to education.[^38] More precisely that in order to reform the school system and the curriculum during 1960s and 1970s it was asked to the leading mathematicians how to teach mathematics and founding the educational system on set theory seemed to be the way to go.[^39] This led to a generation of un-prepared teachers, a generation of low-quality educational material and ultimately poor education.

> We actually don't know how people learn mathematics, and an experimental approach to understand how to teach mathematics should be promoted.

— Cedric Villani

A few such examples are investment in [mathematics education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematics_education "Mathematics education") [^40] and by the [Simons Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simons_Foundation "Simons Foundation").[^41]

Any exact science contains typically two cognitive processes: an [inductive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inductive_reasoning "Inductive reasoning") one (i.e. from experimental evidence to abstraction) and a [deductive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deductive "Deductive") one (i.e. from [axioms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axioms "Axioms") to [theorems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theorems "Theorems") and [proofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_proof "Mathematical proof")). Both of them are key in the [pedagogical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedagogical "Pedagogical") process and more precisely the deductive one is typical of pure mathematics whereas the inductive one is key to mathematics education and to [experimental mathematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_mathematics "Experimental mathematics").

The [covid pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covid_pandemic "Covid pandemic") led also to the so called global learning crisis [^42] with adverse affects on all [scientific education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_education "Scientific education").

As of 2026, major commercially available LLM can reproduce multiple proofs of classical theorems, other LLMs can have very high scores in [mathematical olympiads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_olympiads "Mathematical olympiads") [^43] reflecting average high school mathematics. This technology, even without being able to produce novelty, has the potential to ideally transform education but it is currently not the case.

## In artificial intelligence

As of 2026 it may be feasible in some cases to automatically create large databases of mathematical knowledge [^44] (i.e. automating aspects of the experimental process) and automatically find patterns across large sets of data with [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") to derive mathematical [conjectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjectures "Conjectures") (i.e. automating aspects of the [inductive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_induction "Mathematical induction") process).[^45] Then, putative proofs generated by artificial intelligence can be verified via [proof assistant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proof_assistant "Proof assistant") tools such as [Lean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lean_\(proof_assistant\) "Lean (proof assistant)"), and large supporting databases of theorems [^46] (automating the [deductive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deductive "Deductive") process). Some mathematicians believe that these approaches may become mainstream in the 21st century.[^47] [^48] [^49]
