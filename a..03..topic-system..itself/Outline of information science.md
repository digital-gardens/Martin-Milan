The following [outline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outline_\(list\) "Outline (list)") is provided as an overview of and topical guide to information science:

**[Information science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_science "Information science")** – [interdisciplinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdisciplinarity "Interdisciplinarity") field primarily concerned with the analysis, collection, [classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorization "Categorization"), manipulation, storage, [retrieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_retrieval "Information retrieval") and dissemination of [information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information "Information").[^1] Practitioners within the field study the application and usage of knowledge in [organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization "Organization"), along with the interaction between people, organizations and any existing [information systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_system "Information system"), with the aim of creating, replacing, improving or understanding information systems.

## What type of thing is information science?

Information science can be described as all of the following:

- An [academic discipline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_discipline "Academic discipline") –
- A branch of [science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science "Science") –
	- An [applied science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_science "Applied science") –
		- An [action research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_research "Action research") –

- [International Federation for Information and Documentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_for_Information_and_Documentation "International Federation for Information and Documentation")
- History of information representation and retrieval
	- Has undergone four phases of development [^2]
		1. Increased Demand (1940s–early 1950s) ([Information explosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_explosion "Information explosion"))
				2. Rapid Growth (1950s–1980s) (the emergence of computers and systems such as [Dialog (online database)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialog_\(online_database\) "Dialog (online database)"))
				3. Demystification Phase (1980s–1990s) (systems developed for end-user searching)
				4. The Networked Era (1990s–Present) (search engines such as [AltaVista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AltaVista "AltaVista") and [Google](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google "Google"))

## Research methods of information science

- [Archival research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archival_research "Archival research") – facts or factual evidences from a variety of records are compiled.
- [Computational complexity and structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_complexity_and_structure?action=edit&redlink=1 "Computational complexity and structure (page does not exist)") – algorithmic and graphic methods are used to explore the complexity of information systems, retrieval and storage.
- [Content analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Content_analysis "Content analysis") – studies how people communicate by analyzing the contents of books and mass media as well as the messages people talk or write about.
- [Case study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_study "Case study") – specific set of circumstances or a group (the 'case') is analyzed according to a specific goal of study. Generally, case studies are used to characterize a trend or development; they have weak generalizability.
- [Discourse analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discourse_analysis "Discourse analysis") – analyzing written, oral, and sign language use
- [Historical method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_method "Historical method") – involves a continuous and systematic search for the information and knowledge about past events related to the life of a person, a group, society, or the world.
- [Interviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interviews "Interviews") – researchers obtain data by interviewing people. If the interview is non-structured, the researcher leaves it to the interviewee (also referred to as the respondent or the informant) to guide the conversation.
- [Life history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_history_\(sociology\) "Life history (sociology)") – study of the personal life of a person. Through a series of interviews, the researcher can probe into the decisive moments in their life or the various influences on their life.
- [Longitudinal study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitudinal_study "Longitudinal study") – extensive examination of a specific group over a long period of time.
- [Observation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation "Observation") – using data from the senses, one records information about a social phenomenon or behavior. Qualitative research relies heavily on observation, although it is in a highly disciplined form.
- [Participant observation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Participant_observation "Participant observation") – involves researchers going into the field (usually a community), living with the people for some time, and participating in their activities in order to know and feel their culture.
